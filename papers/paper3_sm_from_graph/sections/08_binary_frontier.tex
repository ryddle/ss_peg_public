\section{The Binary/Ternary Frontier}\label{sec:binary_frontier}

A systematic scan of 65 observables over all $R_{EE}$-power tower combinations reveals a sharp structural divide between quantities that are \textbf{pure powers of $R_{EE}$} and those that \textbf{require the SU(3) spectral ratio $\Rthree$}. This divide---the \textbf{binary/ternary frontier}---is one of the most striking structural patterns in the SS-PEG program. The SU(2) $\times$ SU(3) bifundamental structure of the Standard Model~\cite{connes1994noncommutative,chamseddine1997spectral} is encoded in the E--E subgraph $K_2 \square K_3$.

\subsection{The $R_{EE}$ Power Tower}\label{sec:ree_tower}

The E--E subgraph ratio $R_{EE} = 1/\sqrt{2}$ generates a \textbf{power tower}:
\begin{equation}
R_{EE}^n = (1/\sqrt{2})^n = 2^{-n/2}, \qquad n \in \mathbb{Z}.
\end{equation}

This power tower spans a range of $10^{10}$ in magnitude for $n \in [-20, +20]$. We searched for observables that match pure powers of $R_{EE}$ within various tolerances.

\subsection{Pure Binary Observables}\label{sec:pure_binary}

\textbf{Two observables are pure powers of $R_{EE}$ at sub-$0.5\%$ precision:}

\begin{enumerate}
\item $|V_{tb}| = R_{EE}^0 = 1$ at $0.088\%$ error (essentially exactly 1, as expected for the largest CKM element)
\item $J_{CP}^{\mathrm{CKM}} = R_{EE}^{30} = 2^{-15} = 1/32768$ at $0.485\%$ error (the Jarlskog invariant, discussed in Section~\ref{sec:jarlskog})
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{One observable is near-binary:}

\begin{itemize}
\item $\sin^2\theta_{13}^{\mathrm{PMNS}} \approx R_{EE}^{11} = 2^{-11/2} = 0.02157$ at $0.68\%$ error (close to binary, but not exact)
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Composite Binary Relations}\label{sec:composite_binary}

Beyond the pure-binary observables, we found \textbf{66 composite relations} that are binary at sub-percent precision. These are products and ratios of observables that combine to form pure powers of $R_{EE}$:

These 66 relations reveal \textbf{hidden SU(2) constraints} connecting mass ratios, mixing angles, and couplings that are invisible when observables are analyzed individually. The SU(2) sector---encoded in the E--E subgraph $K_2 \square K_3$---imposes constraints that span across the quark and lepton sectors.

\subsection{The Binary/Ternary Frontier}\label{sec:frontier}

The structural divide between binary and ternary quantities is sharp:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Binary side:} 2 pure-binary observables + 1 near-binary + 66 composite binary relations. These quantities are determined entirely by the SU(2) sector (the E--E subgraph $K_2 \square K_3$).
\item \textbf{Ternary side:} The remaining observables require $\Rthree$ (the SU(3) spectral ratio). These quantities encode the SU(3) structure of the gauge group.
\end{itemize}

The frontier is not arbitrary. It reflects the \textbf{bifundamental structure} of the Standard Model: quarks live in the $(2, 3)$ representation of SU(2) $\times$ SU(3), and the E--E subgraph $K_2 \square K_3$ encodes precisely this bifundamental structure. The binary relations are the ``shadow'' of this bifundamental structure, visible in observables that are dominated by the SU(2) sector.

\subsection{Summary}\label{sec:binary_summary}

The $R_{EE}$ power tower scan reveals a binary/ternary frontier:
\begin{itemize}
\item 2 pure-binary observables at sub-$0.5\%$ ($|V_{tb}|$, $J_{CP}$)
\item 1 near-binary ($\sin^2\theta_{13} \approx R_{EE}^{11}$)
\item 66 composite binary relations at sub-percent precision
\item Hidden SU(2) constraints connecting quark and lepton sectors
\end{itemize}

The frontier reflects the bifundamental structure of the SM: the E--E subgraph $K_2 \square K_3$ encodes the $(2, 3)$ representation, and its binary shadow appears in observables dominated by the SU(2) sector.
