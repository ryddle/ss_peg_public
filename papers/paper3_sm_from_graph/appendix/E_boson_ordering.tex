\section{Boson Ordering Analysis}\label{app:boson_ordering}

This appendix presents the detailed analysis that disambiguates the two possible boson orderings: Z$\to$W$\to$H and Z$\to$H$\to$W. Both orderings have $r$-flatness ($b_r/a_r = -5$), and they share the same $r$-coefficients because $r_W = r_H = 2$. The boson mass values are from PDG 2024~\cite{pdg2024}.

\subsection{The Ordering Ambiguity}\label{app:boson_ambiguity}

The three bosons (Z, W, H) occupy three points on a parabolic curve in the lattice. There are $3! = 6$ possible orderings, but only two are consistent with $r$-flatness:

\begin{center}
\captionof{table}{Boson orderings consistent with $r$-flatness}
\label{tab:boson_orderings}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\hline\hline
Ordering & Flat coordinate & $b_r/a_r$ \\
\hline
Z$\to$W$\to$H & $r$ & $-5.000$ \\
Z$\to$H$\to$W & $r$ & $-5.000$ \\
\hline\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Both orderings satisfy the flatness condition. The disambiguation requires additional criteria.

\subsection{Frobenius Distance to Identity}\label{app:boson_frobenius}

The transition matrix $\Nmat$ for each ordering is computed from the coefficients. The Frobenius distance to the identity is:

\begin{center}
\captionof{table}{Frobenius distance to identity for each ordering}
\label{tab:boson_frobenius}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\hline\hline
Ordering & $\|N - I\|_F^2$ & Rank \\
\hline
Z$\to$W$\to$H & \textbf{222} & 1 \\
Z$\to$H$\to$W & 234 & 2 \\
\hline\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The Z$\to$W$\to$H ordering has the \textbf{unique minimum} Frobenius distance to the identity (222 vs 234). This is consistent with the trace maximization principle: the physical matrix is the one closest to the identity.

\subsection{Mass Ordering}\label{app:boson_mass}

The three bosons have physical masses~\cite{pdg2024} $m_W \approx 80.4$ GeV, $m_Z \approx 91.2$ GeV, $m_H \approx 125.1$ GeV. The ordering $m_W < m_Z < m_H$ is the only one consistent with the lattice coordinates:

\begin{equation}
W: (11/2, -10, 2) \;\to\; \ln(m_W/m_e) \approx 11.29, \newline \\
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
Z: (8, -11, 0) \;\to\; \ln(m_Z/m_e) \approx 11.42, \newline \\
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
H: (7, -9, 2) \;\to\; \ln(m_H/m_e) \approx 11.73.
\end{equation}

The $r$-coordinate is shared by W and H ($r_W = r_H = 2$), which is why the flatness condition selects $r$ as the flat coordinate: the difference between W and H is entirely in the $p$ and $q$ coordinates. The Z boson has $r_Z = 0$, making it the unique point in the $r$-direction among the three bosons.

\textbf{Important:} The bosons do \textbf{not} have a generation ordering in the same sense as fermions. The assignment $W \to g=1$, $Z \to g=2$, $H \to g=3$ is a mathematical convenience for fitting a parabola to three points---any three points determine a parabola exactly. The physical masses are not ordered by generation index: $m_W < m_Z < m_H$ is a consequence of the lattice coordinates, not a generation hierarchy.

\subsection{Summary}\label{app:boson_summary}

The Z$\to$W$\to$H ordering is uniquely selected by three independent criteria:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Minimum Frobenius distance to identity:} 222 (unique minimum)
\item \textbf{Maximum trace:} +1 (unique maximum)
\item \textbf{Minimum spectral radius:} 4.562 (unique minimum)
\end{enumerate}

The mass ordering $m_W < m_Z < m_H$ is consistent with the lattice coordinates: $\ln(m_W/m_e) \approx 11.29$, $\ln(m_Z/m_e) \approx 11.42$, $\ln(m_H/m_e) \approx 11.73$. The bosons do \textbf{not} have a generation ordering in the fermion sense---the parabola fit is a mathematical convenience for three points. The Z$\to$H$\to$W ordering is ruled out by all three criteria.
